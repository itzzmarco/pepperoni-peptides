# Viral Peptides Outrun Their Human Evidence

Of the roughly 50 compounds in the source document, only seven trending molecules are FDA-approved drugs: semaglutide, tirzepatide, tesamorelin, somatropin, bremelanotide (PT-141), elamipretide (SS-31) and afamelanotide (Scenesse). Each is approved for a narrow indication that rarely matches the viral use, and most of the injectables driving Reddit and YouTube traffic (TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, IGF-1 LR3, injectable GHK-Cu, AHK-Cu, Dihexa, FOXO4-DRI and every branded blend) have no published human efficacy or safety data at all. As of October 26, 2026, no peptide has been added to FDA's 503A Bulks List: the April 2026 removal of 12 peptides from Category 2 and the July 2026 advisory votes for six of them changed nothing legally, and an interim enforcement-discretion policy has sat unpublished at the White House since October 14 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)). The source document gets the broad 2026 arc right, but its regulatory timeline predates the July advisory meeting: it wrongly lists CJC-1295 among the April removals, places BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and Epitalon in a February 2027 review they already had, and never says that emideltide (DSIP) was rejected. Its clinical sections mix confirmed trial data with forum claims presented as findings; BPC-157-induced anhedonia, Dihexa's "neuroplasticity window" and a "72-week" retatrutide Phase 2 result have no support or are simply wrong. Nearly every trending injectable is prohibited at all times for drug-tested athletes under WADA's S0, S2 or S4 classes, and "not named" on the List does not mean permitted. The best-documented harms are not pharmacological: a preprint of about 6,300 grey-market test reports found 42% failing a compounding-grade benchmark and 10% of TB-500 samples missing the labeled peptide ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202604.1748)), FDA has documented 5- to 20-fold overdoses from unit-versus-milliliter confusion ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-patients-dosing-errors-associated-compounded)), and endotoxin-contaminated IV glutathione sickened at least 30 patients in 2026 ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-reminds-compounders-not-use-dietary-supplement-grade-glutathione-injectables)). A consumer app should therefore lead with product identity, sterility, dose math and red-flag symptoms, date-stamp every legal-status label, and present "no data" as unknown risk rather than as safety.

## Removing peptides from FDA's risk list legalized nothing in 2026

The question every user asks, "is this peptide legal now?", turns on a three-door test in Section 503A of the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. A licensed pharmacy may compound from a bulk ingredient only if it meets a USP/NF monograph, is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or appears on the 503A Bulks List created by rulemaking ([FDA PCAC briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193342/download)). While that list is built, FDA sorts nominated substances into interim categories: **Category 1** substances may be compounded under enforcement discretion, **Category 2** substances present significant safety risks and may not be compounded, and **Category 3** substances lack enough information to evaluate ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FDA-Announces-Removal-of-12-Peptides-from-Category-2-and-Schedules-PCAC-Meetings)). Three details trip up nearly every secondary source, the user's document included. Categories are specific to 503A pharmacies or 503B outsourcing facilities, so ipamorelin, GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 sit in Category 2 only under 503B, and GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are Category 3 under 503A ([FDA 503A list, May 14, 2026](https://www.fda.gov/media/94155/download)). A substance whose nomination is withdrawn falls into no category at all, so Category 1's safe harbor does not reach it and none of the three statutory doors opens ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FDA-Announces-Removal-of-12-Peptides-from-Category-2-and-Schedules-PCAC-Meetings)). And since January 7, 2025, FDA no longer assigns new nominations to any category ([Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney](https://www.bipc.com/fda-voted-yes-on-six-peptides-now-what-the-regulatory-road-ahead)).

FDA moved about 19 popular peptides into Category 2 on September 29, 2023, citing immunogenicity from aggregation, peptide-related impurities and thin human safety data ([FDA Category 2 page](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks); [Boesen & Snow](https://www.boesensnowlaw.com/blog/fda-advances-peptide-compounding-review-category-2-removals-announced-and-pcac-hearing-confirmed/)). The first withdrawals came a year later: on September 20, 2024, **AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, thymosin alpha-1 and Selank** left Category 2 because their nominators withdrew ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/de/perspectives/2024/10/fda-certain-peptide-bulk-drug-substances-category-2-503a-bulks-pcac)). The advisory committee then voted overwhelmingly against listing what it reviewed: ibutamoren (MK-677) 1–13, ipamorelin 0–12 and kisspeptin-10 0–11 in October 2024 ([PCAC minutes, Oct 29, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/185412/download)), and every form of CJC-1295 0–13 or 1–12, AOD-9604 0–12 and thymosin alpha-1 4–17 in December 2024 ([PCAC minutes, Dec 4, 2024](https://fda.gov/media/185642/download)). Those peptides have sat in legal limbo ever since.

2026 brought a political reversal without a legal one. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. previewed changes for "approximately 14 peptides" on *The Joe Rogan Experience* on February 27 ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FDA-Announces-Removal-of-12-Peptides-from-Category-2-and-Schedules-PCAC-Meetings); [NPR](https://npr.org/2026/03/31/nx-s1-5768206/peptides-rfk-fda-compounding-pharmacies)). On April 15 FDA announced that **12 peptides** would leave Category 2 after nominators withdrew, effective about April 22: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, emideltide (DSIP), Semax and Epitalon for a July advisory meeting, and LL-37, dihexa, injectable GHK-Cu, PEG-MGF and Melanotan II for a later one ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2026/05/fda-signals-potentially-evolving-stance-toward-compounding-of-certain-peptides); [Federal Register 2026-07361](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/16/2026-07361/pharmacy-compounding-advisory-committee-notice-of-meeting-establishment-of-a-public-docket-request)). Every law firm that analyzed the move read it the same way: removal "does not, by itself" make a substance compoundable ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2026/05/fda-signals-potentially-evolving-stance-toward-compounding-of-certain-peptides); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)). FDA reconstituted the Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee (PCAC) on June 29, with roughly half its members reportedly tied to peptide businesses ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)), and ABC News reported that six of the eight recently appointed members run clinics that offer peptides ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/fda-advisers-narrowly-vote-add-6-peptides-drug/story?id=135064915)). On July 23–24 that panel recommended six of seven peptides for the 503A list by narrow margins, overriding FDA staff who opposed all seven: **BPC-157, KPV and TB-500 each 8–6–1, MOTS-c 7–5–2, Semax 8–5–1 and Epitalon 7–4–1**, while **emideltide failed by one vote** (6 yes, 7 no) ([McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/bulk-list-bound-pcac-backs-majority-of-peptides-in-two-day-public-meeting/); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-panel-backs-6-peptides-for-compounding)). FDA's Russell Wesdyk summed up the staff objection on TB-500: "If you don't know what it is and I don't know what it is, how do I put it on the list?" ([TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/07/23/fda-committee-peptides/)).

The votes are non-binding. Listing requires FDA sign-off and notice-and-comment rulemaking that one attorney estimates at 8–12 months ([PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/fda-votes-loosen-restrictions-four-peptides)) and that ABC reports "can take up to a year" ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/fda-advisers-narrowly-vote-add-6-peptides-drug/story?id=135064915)); Holland & Knight calls a federal pathway "unlikely before 2027" ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)). Even a final listing would not make these peptides supplements or mean FDA found them safe or effective ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Health/fda-advisers-narrowly-vote-add-6-peptides-drug/story?id=135064915)). The only fast route is enforcement discretion. An "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" reached the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs on **October 14, 2026**, reportedly covering BPC-157 among others, but its text, peptide list and conditions are not public and it had not issued by mid-October ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/glp-1s/hhs-sends-peptide-compounding-plan-to-white-house-5-things-to-know/); [American Med Spa Association](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/fda-peptide-interim-access-policy-white-house-review/)). Such a policy would not legalize conduct that violates the statute, would not bind state pharmacy boards and could be withdrawn ([Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptide-enforcement-discretion-policy/)). The Partnership for Safe Medicines petitioned FDA on October 16 to block compounding of all seven July peptides by any route ([PSM](https://www.safemedicines.org/2026/10/psm-files-citizen-petitio.html)), while Hims & Hers and Noom have said they will offer peptides if access eases, in a market analysts put at $2–3 billion ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/glp-1s/hhs-sends-peptide-compounding-plan-to-white-house-5-things-to-know/)). The second advisory meeting, due before the end of February 2027 with no date set, covers **LL-37, GHK-Cu, dihexa, Melanotan II and PEG-MGF** ([FDA PCAC page](https://www.fda.gov/advisory-committees/pharmacy-compounding-advisory-committee/meeting-pharmacy-compounding-advisory-committee)).

GLP-1 drugs run on a separate, tighter track. FDA declared the tirzepatide shortage over on October 2, 2024 and reaffirmed it on December 19, 2024; semaglutide's ended February 21, 2025; and the grace periods for pharmacies and outsourcing facilities closed between February and May 2025 ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize)). On August 27, 2026 the Fifth Circuit affirmed FDA in both Outsourcing Facilities Association cases in unpublished per curiam opinions, holding that any notice-and-comment error was harmless rather than ruling on the compounders' procedural theory ([5th Cir. No. 25-10600](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/25/25-10600.0.pdf); [No. 25-10758](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/unpub/25/25-10758.0.pdf)). Patient-specific 503A compounding survives only where a product is not "essentially a copy" ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/fifth-circuit-upholds-fda-glp1-shortage-decisions/)). FDA calls semaglutide sodium and acetate "different active ingredients" than the approved drugs, states that retatrutide and cagrilintide "cannot be used in compounding under federal law," and had logged **990 adverse-event reports for compounded semaglutide and more than 730 for compounded tirzepatide** by May 31, 2026, with underreporting likely because 503A pharmacies need not report ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss)). It also launched a "green list" import alert for GLP-1 ingredients in September 2025 and proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list in April 2026 ([FDA green list](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-green-list-protect-americans-illegal-imported-glp-1-drug-ingredients); [FDA 503B proposal](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-proposes-exclude-semaglutide-tirzepatide-and-liraglutide-503b-bulks-list)). The SAFE Drugs Act (H.R. 6509), introduced December 9, 2025, would cap "copies" at 20 units per month and tighten outsourcing-facility inspections, but it had not been enacted as of February 2026 ([Dykema](https://dykema.com/news-insights/congress-introduces-safe-drugs-act-of-2025-expanding-fda-oversight-of-compounded-glp-1-drugs-and-telehealth-providers.html)).

The "research use only" label is not a loophole. FDA's March 31, 2026 warning letters to Gram Peptides and Mile High Compounds applied the intended-use standard despite RUO disclaimers, cited a forum "Peptide Cheat Sheet" with weekly dosing as evidence of human use, and treated bacteriostatic water sold alongside peptides as a drug in its own right ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026); [FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/mile-high-compounds-llc-721600-03312026)). Criminal exposure is real: a seller of Chinese-sourced semaglutide and tirzepatide labeled "for research purposes only" was sentenced to **21 months** on July 23, 2026 ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdmi/pr/2026_0723_Piper_Sentencing_PR)), echoing 2014 and 2015 prosecutions of RUO peptide sites ([DOJ 2014](https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdnc/pr/two-charlotte-men-plead-guilty-selling-misbranded-drugs); [DOJ 2015](https://www.justice.gov/usao-md/pr/owner-bodybuilding-drug-companies-indicted-selling-misbranded-drugs)). Lilly filed six lawsuits against retatrutide sellers in August 2026 and reported more than 200 referrals to regulators and prosecutors ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/peptides-at-a-crossroads-what-wellness-providers-need-to-know)).

For drug-tested athletes the answer is simpler and stricter. WADA's S0 class prohibits at all times "any pharmacological substance" not addressed elsewhere on the List and lacking approval by any government health authority, and names BPC-157 explicitly ([WADA 2026 List](https://www.segas.gr/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026list_en_final_clean_september_2025.pdf)). S2 names kisspeptin (in males), GH fragments including AOD-9604 and hGH 176-191, GHRH analogues including CJC-1295, sermorelin and tesamorelin, secretagogues including ibutamoren and ipamorelin, GHRPs including GHRP-2 and GHRP-6, and growth factors including IGF-1 analogues, mechano growth factors and "Thymosin-ß4 and its derivatives e.g. TB-500"; S4.4 names MOTS-c ([WADA 2026 List](https://www.segas.gr/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026list_en_final_clean_september_2025.pdf)). Semaglutide and tirzepatide remain permitted but sit in WADA's 2026 Monitoring Program, and the 2027 List, approved September 10, 2026, leaves them unchanged ([WADA Monitoring Program](https://www.tennisireland.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-Monitoring-Programme.pdf); [World Rugby](https://www.world.rugby/keep-rugby-clean/news/1048285/wada-publishes-2027-prohibited-list)). Unnamed and unapproved peptides such as retatrutide, KPV, Melanotan II, dihexa and FOXO4-DRI most likely fall under S0, while compounds approved somewhere (Semax and Selank in Russia, Cerebrolysin in the EU, thymosin alpha-1 abroad) are genuinely ambiguous. That is a reading of the List's text, not a WADA ruling, so the app should send athletes to Global DRO or their anti-doping agency rather than display "permitted." Separately, IV infusions over 100 mL per 12 hours outside hospital or clinical settings are prohibited under M2.2, which captures many glutathione, NAD+ and carnitine drips ([WADA 2026 List](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/trb-2026-25.pdf)).

## Seven approved drugs anchor a field of mostly untested molecules

The fact tables below use a five-tier evidence scale: **1** = approved for a specific indication; **2** = randomized trials exist but the drug is unapproved for the popular use; **3** = early human data from small or uncontrolled studies; **4** = preclinical only; **5** = unstudied. Where a compound's best evidence covers one use and its viral use is another, both tiers appear. The source document uses a different, partly inverted 1–5 scale (it rates glutathione "Trial-Backed (4 of 5)" and AHK-Cu "Preclinical (2 of 5)"), so its numbers should not be copied into the app. Doses appear only where a label or published trial states them; community protocols are excluded because no source validates them. Half-lives are human values unless marked, and "none" means no published figure was found, which the app should display as "unknown," never as short or harmless.

Across the field the pattern is stark. Seven trending molecules hold US approvals, and a further handful hold approvals abroad: thymalfasin (thymosin alpha-1) for hepatitis, aviptadil-phentolamine for erectile dysfunction in the UK, Cerebrolysin in the EU, Semax and reportedly Selank in Russia, oral glutathione in Japan, and GHRP-2 as a one-time Japanese diagnostic. Roughly 20 compounds sit at tier 4 or 5 for their popular use. Even the approved drugs rarely match their viral use: tesamorelin's label says it is "not indicated for weight loss" ([Egrifta WR label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/022505s020lbl.pdf)), PT-141 is "not indicated" in men ([Vyleesi label](https://nctr-crs.fda.gov/fdalabel/services/spl/set-ids/8c9607a2-5b57-4a59-b159-cf196deebdd9/spl-doc)), and elamipretide's Barth syndrome approval rests on open-label strength gains after its randomized phase missed both primary endpoints ([Forzinity PI](https://www.drugs.com/pro/forzinity.html)).

### Weight and metabolic: real Phase 3 data, and the most counterfeits

The metabolic family is the only one with Phase 3-grade evidence and the only one where grey-market vials imitate real drugs, which makes it both the best-evidenced and the most counterfeited category. Retatrutide's results are real but need two caveats in the app. The headline 28.3–28.7% losses are "efficacy estimand" results that assume people stayed on treatment, and treatment-regimen results run 3–5 points lower ([Lilly TRIUMPH-1](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss)). And at 12 mg, one in five TRIUMPH-4 participants had dysesthesia and 18.2% quit for adverse events, so the top dose is not simply "best" ([Lilly TRIUMPH-4](https://investor.lilly.com/node/53536/pdf)). The post-retatrutide contenders are weaker than forum talk suggests: CagriSema failed non-inferiority against tirzepatide, EloraTZP's tolerability is worse than tirzepatide's rather than better, and AT7687 has no human weight data. The older "fat burners" fail outright: AOD-9604 lost its largest trial, and 5-Amino-1MQ is not even a peptide.

### Growth hormone axis: biomarkers rise, strength and function do not

GH-axis peptides reliably raise growth hormone and IGF-1, but no trial shows the outcomes users buy them for. MK-677 has the most human data of any unapproved secretagogue, and it is a cautionary tale: two years of daily dosing added 1.1 kg of fat-free mass with no change in strength or function, raised fasting glucose, and a hip-fracture trial was stopped early for congestive heart failure ([Nass 2008](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/effects-of-an-oral-ghrelin-mimetic-on-body-composition-and-clinic/); [FDA ibutamoren briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/182087/download)). The approved labels for somatropin, tesamorelin and mecasermin show the class risks that plausibly extend to every compound raising GH or IGF-1: glucose intolerance, edema and joint pain, carpal tunnel, and malignancy contraindications. Reasonable "ask a doctor first or avoid" groups, label-derived for the approved drugs and extrapolated for the rest, are people with active or past cancer, pregnancy, diabetes or proliferative retinopathy, pituitary disease, heart failure, acute critical illness, and adolescents. Using HGH itself for anti-aging is not a gray area: distributing it for any use other than an authorized medical condition is a federal crime carrying up to five years ([21 U.S.C. §333(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/333)).

### Repair and immune: one randomized BPC-157 abstract, zero TB-500 humans

BPC-157 has more human data than the source document says, but not the data users assume. FDA's July 2026 review found five clinical studies totaling about 108 subjects, only one randomized (a 53-person ulcerative colitis enema study published as a meeting abstract, with a confidence interval crossing zero), and none in tendon, ligament or muscle injury; an orthopedic systematic review states flatly that "no study assessed the safety or adverse events of BPC-157 in humans" ([FDA BPC-157 briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193343/download); [HSS Journal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12313605/)). Over 80% of BPC-157 publications trace to one Croatian group ([Józwiak et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8247/18/10/1451/html)), a single-lab concentration that recurs with thymalin (Khavinson and Kuznik) and nasal VIP (Shoemaker) and that the app could surface as a standing quality caveat. The cancer concern behind "angiogenic" peptides is a plausible, unstudied hazard, since no carcinogenicity study exists for BPC-157, TB-500 or KPV; the neuropsychiatric claims (anhedonia, hypomania) have no support in FDA's adverse-event database or the literature. TB-500 is the starkest case: no human has ever been studied on it, and in the one direct test the parent molecule showed no wound-healing activity while a breakdown fragment did ([Rahaman 2024](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/simultaneous-quantification-of-tb-500-and-its-metabolites-in-in-v/)). The overlooked standout is ARA-290, which has two randomized Phase 2 trials. Among immune peptides, the approved-abroad thymosin alpha-1 lost its largest trial, systemic VIP failed Phase 3, and LL-37's viral 2026 study measured the body's own levels rather than testing a supplement.

### Skin, tan and sexual health: thin topical data and case-report harms

The most viral compound in the whole ecosystem rests on the thinnest base. GHK-Cu's widely quoted "70% vs 50% vs 40%" collagen figures come from a 20-person, one-month thigh-skin pilot without placebo; the 12-week facial studies were conference abstracts, one without a control group; and the only peer-reviewed randomized trial with objective endpoints (13 completers after laser resurfacing) found no objective difference, only higher patient satisfaction ([BioImpacts review](https://bi.tbzmed.ac.ir/FullHtml/bi-30071); [Miller 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16847171/)). Route matters legally as well as scientifically: topical GHK-Cu is Category 1, injectable GHK-Cu is in no category and cannot be compounded, so the app needs two separate entries. Melanotan II's harms come from case reports: eruptive and dysplastic moles, several melanomas, priapism, rhabdomyolysis, renal infarction and PRES. Causation for melanoma is unproven because most cases involved fair-skinned sunbed users ([Hueso-Gabriel 2012](https://actasdermo.org/en-eruptive-dysplastic-nevi-following-melanotan-articulo-S1578219012001357); [Mertens 2020](https://nvdv.nl/storage/app/media/dermatologische-risicogcos-bij-ongereguleerd-gebruik-van-alfa-melanocyt-stimulerend-hormoon-m-mertens-ntvdv-2020-09.pdf)), so the app should say "reported in case reports, causation not established," not "causes melanoma" and not "safe." The approved melanocortin relatives show what real oversight looks like: afamelanotide requires a clinician-placed implant and twice-yearly skin exams, and PT-141 causes nausea in 40% of users and raises blood pressure.

### Mind and mood: Russian registrations that fail Western evidence standards

None of the nootropic peptides is FDA-approved, and none has evidence for the uses that drive Reddit interest: replacing stimulants, reversing PSSD, curing post-opioid anhedonia or boosting cognition in healthy people. Semax and Selank are registered in Russia, but their trials are small (20–110 people), mostly Russian-language or abstract-only, often lack placebo arms, and have no independent replication; FDA found "insufficient evidence" for every Semax indication it reviewed and no human pharmacokinetic studies at all ([FDA Semax briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193348/download)). The PCAC's 8–5 vote for Semax was an access vote, not an efficacy finding. Dihexa's rodent case rests on papers that carry journal expressions of concern for possible image manipulation ([Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/09/24/four-papers-by-athira-ceo-earn-expressions-of-concern/)), and its HGF/MET-pathway relative fosgonimeton failed a 312-patient Alzheimer's trial ([Athira](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1620463/000095017024102788/atha-ex99_1.htm)). Cerebrolysin, the only EU-approved compound here, did not reduce deaths in acute stroke and was associated with more non-fatal serious adverse events in the Cochrane analysis ([Cochrane](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD007026.pub7/media/CDSR/CD007026/CD007026.pdf)); its label interaction with antidepressants and MAO inhibitors matters precisely because many of its Reddit users are treating depression ([SmPC](https://www.everpharma.com/pol/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/01/Cerebrolysin-Charakterystyka-Produktu-Leczniczego.pdf)).

### Longevity and mitochondria: one rare-disease approval amid mouse data

## The source document's regulatory timeline froze before July 2026

The source document is a useful map of what is trending and a poor source of facts. Its errors follow five patterns. First, the regulatory timeline appears to come from material written before the July 23–24 PCAC meeting, so it puts already-decided peptides in a future review, misses the emideltide rejection and invents a pending CJC-1295 rule. Second, it flattens FDA's categories, calling 503B-only listings "Category 2," placing withdrawn substances in "active" Category 2 and putting sermorelin, L-carnitine and B12 in a Category 1 they do not occupy. Third, it presents forum anecdotes in the same voice as trial data, especially for BPC-157, Dihexa, CJC-1295 + ipamorelin and Cerebrolysin. Fourth, it misattributes numbers: retatrutide's Phase 2 timing, a 58% figure that belongs to a different trial and endpoint, an NAFLD responder cutoff reported as a mean, and background statements in an AHK-Cu paper reported as its findings. Fifth, nearly all of its clinical claims cite a single aggregator (humanpeptides.fyi), and its proprietary "engagement points" cannot be verified; the ranking table even contradicts its own numbers, ranking glutathione (2,236 points) eighth below AHK-Cu (896 points) in fifth and tesamorelin (1,573) in sixth. ## Contamination, dose math and sterility are the best-documented harms

### Grey-market vials fail on dose and identity more often than on purity

The largest dataset is a not-yet-peer-reviewed April 2026 preprint analyzing **6,285 voluntary test reports covering 203 vendors and 14 compounds**. **41.6%** of samples failed a compounding-grade benchmark (90–110% of labeled amount, ≥98% purity) and **71.1%** failed a manufacturing-grade one (95–105%, ≥99.5%) ([Mendias & Awan preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202604.1748)). The dominant problem was dose, not purity: median purity was 99.8%, but abundance ranged widely (IQR 95–109%), and only 13.8% of tirzepatide samples passed both manufacturing thresholds. Identity failures, where the labeled peptide was not found at all, hit **10.0% of TB-500, 9.1% of CJC-1295, 3.6% of tesamorelin and 3.5% of BPC-157** samples. Of the 243 samples tested for endotoxin, 15% had measurable levels, and purity did not predict endotoxin (R² < 0.01). The data have real limits: submissions were voluntary and non-random, and the thresholds are the authors' own rather than regulatory standards. Peer-reviewed work points the same way. Test purchases of illegal online semaglutide found endotoxin of 2.16–8.95 EU/mg and 28.56–38.69% more drug than labeled, and none of three Ozempic pen orders ever arrived ([Ashraf et al., JMIR](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e65440/PDF)). FDA has also seized counterfeit Ozempic in the US market and warns it "could contain the wrong ingredients" ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/fdas-concerns-unapproved-glp-1-drugs-used-weight-loss)). Even nominators' own paperwork fails basic identity checks: the TB-500 certificate of analysis described the acetate while the nomination was for the free base, with a formula matching neither ([FDA TB-500 briefing](https://www.fda.gov/media/193349/download)). The app's core message follows directly: a certificate's purity percentage says nothing about dose accuracy, identity, sterility or endotoxin.

### Glutathione shows licensed pharmacies fail too

On August 27, 2026, FDA reported **at least 30 patients** with fever, chills, pain, dizziness, signs of shock and sepsis-like symptoms after IV glutathione, several traced to Medisca lot #229536, which was labeled dietary-supplement grade. FDA called supplement-grade ingredient "not an appropriate ingredient to make injectable drugs," told compounders not to "rely on testing alone," and advised patients and clinicians to ask where the glutathione came from and report problems to MedWatch ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-reminds-compounders-not-use-dietary-supplement-grade-glutathione-injectables)). CDC counted more than 25 ill people in at least four states, at least three hospitalized and one in intensive care with shock and abnormal clotting ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthcare-associated-infections/current-situation/index.html)). One recalled lot had been distributed to 14 states ([FDA recall](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/optimal-balance-pharmacy-issues-voluntary-nationwide-recall-certain-lots-compounded-glutathione-200)), and a later recall involved two ER visits after a product intended for subcutaneous use was given intravenously ([MedShadow](https://medshadow.org/drug-updates-recalls/fda-recalls-and-warnings/third-glutathione-injection-recall-endotoxin-contamination/)). The same failure happened in 2019, when FDA found endotoxin "as high as five times the appropriate limit" in supplement-labeled glutathione powder ([FDA 2019](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-highlights-concerns-using-dietary-ingredient-glutathione-compound-sterile-injectables)). The lesson for the app is that the danger is structural, not a single bad lot, and that fever or rigors within hours of an injection is the signature to watch for.

### Bacteriostatic water preserves; it does not sterilize

Bacteriostatic Water for Injection contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, has a pH of about 5.7 (range 4.5–7.0), is a multiple-dose product, and is labeled "only for diluting or dissolving drugs" for IV, IM or subcutaneous injection. It is "NOT FOR USE IN NEONATES" because benzyl alcohol is toxic to newborns, it can cause hemolysis if injected IV without a solute, and its label says not to store reconstituted drug solutions "unless otherwise directed by the manufacturer of the solute" ([Pfizer label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=4666); [label reproduction](https://medlibrary.org/lib/rx/meds/bacteriostatic-water-4/page/2/)). Approved peptides take benzyl alcohol seriously too: elamipretide's label warns of fatal "gasping syndrome" in neonates ([Forzinity PI](https://www.drugs.com/pro/forzinity.html)). A bacteriostatic preservative inhibits microbial growth; it cannot rescue a contaminated powder or non-sterile technique, an inference from the label's description of benzyl alcohol as a preservative rather than a sterilant. The supply of diluent is itself unreliable: Amazon listings largely disappeared around March 31, 2026 with no public explanation, and in vendor-commissioned testing just before, 9 of 11 Amazon brands failed pH or benzyl-alcohol specifications, though sterility was never tested ([PeptidePriceLab](https://peptidepricelab.com/news/bacteriostatic-water-amazon-removed-2026/)). FDA's March 2026 warning letters treat bacteriostatic water sold with research peptides as an unapproved drug in its own right ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/mile-high-compounds-llc-721600-03312026)).

### Units, milliliters and milligrams cause tenfold errors

FDA's July 2024 alert on compounded semaglutide documents the mechanism. On a U-100 insulin syringe, 5 units equals 0.05 mL, yet patients drew 50 units instead of 5 and received **5 to 20 times** the intended dose; clinicians also wrote 25 units instead of 5 and 20 instead of 2. Reported harms included nausea, vomiting, fainting, dehydration, acute pancreatitis and gallstones, some requiring hospitalization, and FDA urged syringes sized to the dose plus explicit teaching ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-patients-dosing-errors-associated-compounded)). The underlying arithmetic is simple but unforgiving: concentration (mg/mL) equals peptide mass divided by diluent volume, a U-100 syringe holds 100 units per mL, and each unit therefore delivers concentration × 10 micrograms. The same "10 units" delivers a different dose from every vial mixed differently, which is exactly how wrong-vial and wrong-volume errors happen. The illustration below uses one arbitrary 250 mcg target to show the spread; it is not a dose recommendation for any compound.

| Vial contents | Diluent added | Concentration | mcg per unit (U-100) | Units for 250 mcg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2.5 mg/mL | 25 | 10 |
| 10 mg | 2 mL | 5 mg/mL | 50 | 5 |
| 10 mg | 1 mL | 10 mg/mL | 100 | 2.5 |

The calculation also assumes the label is right. Given the preprint's abundance spread (IQR 95–109%), real doses from grey-market vials can differ from calculated ones by 5–10% or more even when the math is perfect ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202604.1748)). An app should therefore store, for every vial, the labeled milligrams, the milliliters of diluent added, the resulting micrograms per unit, the lot and the mixing date, and show doses in both units and micrograms.

### No stability data exist for home-mixed research peptides

Laboratory-supply guidance recommends storing lyophilized peptides at −20 °C away from bright light, keeping them sealed and desiccated (peptides containing Asp, Glu, Lys, Arg or His absorb moisture, and Cys, Met or Trp oxidize easily), warming vials to room temperature before opening, and aliquoting to avoid repeated freeze-thaw. The same vendor says peptides in solution have a "much shorter" shelf life and "does not recommend storing peptides in solution" ([GenScript](https://www.genscript.com/peptide_storage_and_handling.html)). This is research-reagent guidance, not clinical labeling. For manufactured multi-dose vials, CDC and USP <797> set a default discard date of 28 days after opening unless the manufacturer states otherwise ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/injectionsafety/providers/provider_faqs_multivials.html)), and approved peptides set their own limits: elamipretide vials are discarded 8 days after first opening ([Forzinity PI](https://www.drugs.com/pro/forzinity.html)), and Egrifta WR is reconstituted weekly ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001512717/000119312525063760/d944896dex991.htm)). Grey-market peptides have no manufacturer and no stability data, so any community "good for X weeks in the fridge" rule is unvalidated; the app should say so rather than supply a number.

### Injection hygiene, sharps, and the red flags that need emergency care

CDC's injection-safety rules translate directly to home use: use a new sterile needle and syringe every time, never leave a needle in a vial septum, and keep a multi-dose vial to one person ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/injectionsafety/providers/provider_faqs_multivials.html)). Approved incretin labels add "never share pens" ([Wegovy label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031lbl218316s003lbl.pdf)), and a study of Melanotan forum users flagged infection transmission from shared injecting ([Gilhooley 2021](https://karger.com/drm/article-abstract/237/6/995/828180/Melanotan-II-User-Experience-A-Qualitative-Study)). Used sharps go immediately into an FDA-cleared container or a heavy-duty household substitute, never loose into the trash, the toilet or recycling, and full containers (about three-quarters) go to drop-off sites, household hazardous-waste programs or mail-back services; the Safe Needle Disposal hotline is 1-800-643-1643 ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safely-using-sharps-needles-and-syringes-home-work-and-travel/dos-and-donts-proper-sharps-disposal); [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safely-using-sharps-needles-and-syringes-home-work-and-travel/best-way-get-rid-used-needles-and-other-sharps)).

## Conclusion

The 2026 policy churn is producing a status no consumer category has had before: molecules that FDA's own scientists say are not even well characterized, such as TB-500, KPV and MOTS-c, are moving toward pharmacy access on the strength of advisory votes from a panel heavily drawn from peptide clinics. If the interim access policy issues, "compounded by a licensed pharmacy" will become a powerful signal of legitimacy that no efficacy or safety review stands behind. The most useful thing an education app can do is therefore to split what the source document blends into one "classification": **legal to compound**, **shown to work in humans** and **shown to be safe in humans** are three separate fields. For most trending peptides they read "not yet," "no" and "unknown," and they will change on different timelines, the first possibly within weeks and the other two only with trials that nobody is currently running.

The second shift is about where the danger lies. The source document dramatizes speculative pharmacology (tumor "fertilizer," permanent anhedonia, "hardwired" maladaptive circuits), but the harms with real denominators are mundane and preventable: vials that hold the wrong amount or the wrong peptide, endotoxin that purity certificates do not detect, and unit-versus-milligram arithmetic that turns a 5-unit dose into 50. That points the app's highest-yield features away from encyclopedia entries and toward per-vial concentration logging, unit-to-microgram display, lot tracking, symptom check-ins timed to the hours after each injection, and an athlete mode that treats "not named by WADA" as "ask first." Every status label should carry an "as of" date and be refreshed when the interim policy and the February 2027 advisory meeting land, because those two events are the most likely to make this report's regulatory rows obsolete.
